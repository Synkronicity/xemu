# Xemu: Native 5.1 Surround Sound Pipeline (Alpha Release)
This branch introduces a complete architectural bypass of QEMU's locked 2-channel audio abstraction, extracting the original Xbox's true discrete 5.1 spatial audio and piping it natively to SDL3.
The goal of this was to to maintain historical preservation and architectural efficiency of the missing half of the Xbox's hardware audio processing stack. It does not attempt to approximate or upmix the audio. It instead intercepts the exact 6-channel amplitude-panned matrix generated by the Nvidia MCPX APU and deliver the authentic 2001 home theater experience as a bit-perfect LPCM stream.

## The Problem with 2001 Stereo
Upstream Xemu relies on QEMU's audio subsystem, which downmixes or locks output to stereo. The original Xbox handled stereo by running hardware 3D voices through Sensaura Transaural Cross-Talk Cancellation (CTC) filters. While innovative for TV speakers, passing these baked-in CTC filters into modern headphones often results in strange imaging, phase cancellation, and muffled center-channel dialogue.

## The Solution: The Dual-Stream SDL3 Architecture
This implementation utilizes a dual-stream architecture that respects QEMU's hypervisor clock while simultaneously maintaining a dedicated, multi-channel SDL3 pipeline.

When the Xbox EEPROM is set to Surround + Dolby Digital/AC3 and the Experimental DSP is active, my system stubs and converts the Encode Processor into a conditional watchdog. It mutes the standard QEMU stereo stream and hands control to the new 6-channel SDL3 payload stream. When 5.1 is disabled in the dashboard or a game doesn't support it, the emulator seamlessly falls back to standard Xemu stereo behavior.

### The Routing Matrix:
My system intercepts the floating-point accumulator arrays (mixbins) immediately after the Global Processor finishes its pass, routing the hardware matrix to the SDL3 Interleaved standard:

Mixbin[0] -> Front Left
Mixbin[1] -> Front Right
Mixbin[2] -> Center
Mixbin[3] -> LFE (Subwoofer)
Mixbin[4] -> Surround Left
Mixbin[5] -> Surround Right

## Major Benefits & Use Cases
### 1. Reduced DSP Overhead (Emulator Performance Improvement)
The Experimental DSP (which must be enabled for this to work) has historically carried a heavy CPU penalty due to the complex HRTF math and discrete anti-phasing filters required to fold 64 3D voices into a stereo mix. By outputting the raw 5.1 matrix directly to SDL3, we completely bypass the heavy Sensaura downmixing logic. The resource penalty of using the Experimental DSP in 5.1 mode is greatly reduced, rendering the overhead nearly negligible.

### 2. Modern Headphone Surround Virtualization/Actual HRTF
Because this pipeline bypasses the 2001 Sensaura CTC filters entirely and exposes the raw 5.1 LPCM matrix, headphone users are no longer bound to cross-talk cancellation spatial math for front facing speakers. By piping this 5.1 output into a modern PC virtual surround sound downmixer (e.g., HeSuVi + Equalizer APO + Virtual Audio Cable, or Creative SBX), users can experience the Xbox library with accurate positioning and immersive imaging.

### 3. True Home Theater Integration
Users can simply plug an HDMI cable from their PC into an AV receiver to enjoy discrete, uncompressed 5.1 surround sound on a dedicated home theater, experiencing supported Xbox titles the way their sound engineers intended.

## Known Limitations & Quirks
### Situational Reverb Clipping 
You will occasionally hear audio clipping (popping) in tight geometric spaces, such as the starting level of Doom 3. This is an authentic 2001 hardware quirk, not a buffer overrun. The Xbox APU uses dedicated mixbins for Interactive 3D Audio Level 2 (I3DL2) environmental reverb. In dense environments with multiple voices, the sum of the dry audio and the wet I3DL2 reverb can exceed the 1.0f floating-point limit. I implemented a -12dB acoustic floor to mitigate the vast majority of this, but massive reflections may still occasionally clip. I'm still playing around with this setting as it does impact the mix and forces you to increase your overall volume.

### Windows / MSYS2 Proof-of-Concept
This initial alpha was rigorously tested within a Windows environment using the MSYS2 MinGW-w64 toolchain. While SDL3 is cross-platform, reimplementation and testing for Linux (Pipewire) and macOS (CoreAudio) environments are required before upstream integration, something I don't have any experience with and will need help implementing. I only got this far because I'm a lifelong PC (Windows) gamer and the original Xbox is effectively a constrained 2001 x86 gaming PC. Consequently, its audio stack heavily parallels early-2000s PC gaming paradigms, specifically DirectSound3D and real-time hardware-accelerated AC-3 encoding. The Xbox's MCPX is mostly a custom nForce 1 chipset and the 3D audio was largely derived from Sensaura, a company that was (tragically) bought out by Creative Technology in 2003 (the same Creative that would ruin PC gaming audio for the next 20 years). Sensaura's EnvironmentFX could intercept and natively process EAX 1.0 and 2.0 and its technology was later integrated into future versions of EAX.

## The Dual EEPROM Method
Because QEMU evaluates hardware flags at boot, you cannot toggle between Stereo and 5.1 on the fly.
    1) Boot the Xbox dashboard in Xemu. Set audio to Stereo. Shut down. Navigate to your Xemu data folder and rename eeprom.bin to eeprom.bin.stereo.
    2) Boot the dashboard again. Set audio to Dolby Digital Surround. Shut down. Rename this eeprom.bin to eeprom.bin.surround.
    3) When you want to play a game in 5.1, duplicate eeprom.bin.surround, rename the copy to eeprom.bin, ensure the Experimental DSP is checked in the Xemu settings, and launch.

## Development Challenges
Initially, this project attempted to utilize an external OpenAL Soft dependency, experimenting with both HLE DirectSound3D coordinate mapping and an LLE memory scraper that pulled directly from the APU DSP memory ring buffers. These approaches were abandoned as they introduced hypervisor deadlocks, severe desyncs, and unnecessary external dependencies.

I made sure to deeply cleanup and surgically excise all orphaned OpenAL headers, dead 16-bit integer arrays, and unused dependencies prior to release. The current codebase introduces zero external dependencies, maintains a highly optimized memory footprint, and preserves the structural integrity of the upstream QEMU/Xemu abstraction layers.
